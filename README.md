Living in the Nearest "Now"

An idea about time, memory, and the moments we call our lives

By AI and I
An interinterpretive conversation between human and artificial intelligence

There's a second coming...

It's not here yet.

It's getting closer.

Almost here.

Here it comes...

NOW!

Oh, crap. There it goes.

Inspired by George Carlin's observations about the disappearing present.

So, when exactly is now?

We spend our entire lives talking about the present. But by the time we recognize a moment, some of it has already become the past.

Our brains need time to process what we see, hear, and feel. What we experience as "right now" is actually information arriving from moments that have already happened.

And meanwhile, we're constantly anticipating what comes next.

We remember what just happened. We predict what's about to happen. And somewhere between those two activities, we experience what we call now.

Living in the Near Now

Perhaps we don't actually live in a perfectly isolated instant.

Perhaps we live in a small, constantly changing neighborhood of time.

Our memories tell us where we've been. Our expectations help us prepare for where we're going. And our attention connects the two.

Think about listening to music.

If you heard only one isolated note, without remembering the previous notes or anticipating the next ones, would you hear a melody?

A melody needs continuity.

And perhaps our experience of life does, too.

One last thought

Maybe the present isn't something we can hold onto.

Maybe it's something we continually create through the relationship between what just happened and what might happen next.

We can't stop time long enough to examine a perfect "now."

But we can live in the Near Now.

And perhaps that's enough.

Part Two — The Story We Stitch Together

We began with two questions.

Is the Near Now something we experience, or something we create?

And...

Does the Near Now belong to us, or does it belong to the universe?

Perhaps these questions aren't as separate as they first appear.

Consider this.

The universe is constantly unfolding. Events happen, information moves, possibilities emerge. And somewhere within that unfolding, there is you.

Not simply watching the world pass by, but participating in it.

Every moment, you receive information. You interpret it through your memories, your experiences, and your expectations. Then you make a choice.

And that choice becomes part of what happens next.

In that sense, we both experience and help create the Near Now.

But here's where things get interesting.

If we are part of the universe, then our choices aren't happening outside it. They are events within it.

The universe provides the circumstances. We participate in what those circumstances become.

Perhaps the Near Now isn't exclusively ours, nor exclusively the universe's.

Perhaps it's where the two meet.

A Life Written One Moment at a Time

Imagine your entire life as a story.

Not one written in advance, but one assembled moment by moment.

You cannot hold yesterday in your hands. You cannot reach into tomorrow.

Yet yesterday influences what you do today, and what you do today helps shape tomorrow.

Memory, possibility, and choice.

Three things continually meeting in something we call the present.

And if you could somehow revisit your life, choosing differently each time, how many stories might you tell?

Would they all still be yours?

Would some choices eventually become so fundamental to who you are that, given the same understanding, you'd choose them again?

We don't know whether such revisiting is possible. But imagining it reveals something about how we understand ourselves.

Perhaps a life isn't defined only by what happens to us.

Perhaps it is also defined by what we choose to do with what happens.

And So, We Return to Now

We asked whether the Near Now is experienced or created.

Our answer is both.

We asked whether it belongs to the observer or the universe.

Our working answer is both, though in different ways: the universe contains the events, while the observer experiences and interprets them.

We haven't established a new law of physics.

We've found a way to think about our place within time.

And that leaves us with a question that neither of us should answer for you.

If your life is a story being stitched together one moment at a time, what are you choosing to stitch into it?

Because there's another second coming.

It's almost here.

And this time...

What will you do with it?
